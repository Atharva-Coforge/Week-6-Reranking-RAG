## Close notes

This memo is mec-us-0001, v1.0, effective 2025-09-01. Amounts are in USD. It applies to every business unit that keeps monthly books. It is written as a running account rather than a stack of short sections, because the close is one stretch of work and the mistakes happen where one topic runs into the next.

The close period is the calendar month being finalized. A workday is a day the office is open for ordinary business. Weekends and company holidays are not workdays. Lock is the moment that month's records stop moving: no new lines, no edits, and no deletions. A general ledger code classifies the transaction. An authorized role is a job ID allowed to enter or post while the month is still open.

New clerks are often told that the books freeze on the 5th calendar day of the next month. That account is wrong, and it has made teams lock late. The close period is locked on the 5th workday of the following month, and only after the finance manager approves the lock. Dana Okonkwo holds that approval and signs form MEC-3. Dana Okonkwo-Reed sits on the same finance hallway, collects the backup, and cannot approve the lock or sign form MEC-3. After Dana Okonkwo approves and the lock is applied, the period is fixed.

Before that morning, transactions for the close period are entered, reviewed, and reconciled so the balances are complete. An authorized role confirms that every known transaction for the period is recorded. Only these job IDs may enter or post during the close: US-0001, US-0020, US-0984, US-9033, and US-8932. US-0948 is not on that list. Clerks mix US-0948 up with US-0984 because the digits sit next to each other. A line posted by US-0948, or by any job ID outside the list, is not permitted and is reversed when it is found. The entry is flagged and sent to the finance manager, who decides whether it is reversed, moved onto an authorized job ID, or otherwise corrected before lock.

Coding is easy to blur in the same afternoon as the job-ID check. Travel costs are recorded under general ledger code 6100-TRAVEL. They are not recorded under 6110-TRAVEL, a rejected code that still sits on old templates. Unpaid bills are recorded under general ledger code 2100-AP. They are not recorded under 2110-AP. The authorized role applies the code at the time of entry. If the code is wrong and the period is still open, an authorized role corrects it and rechecks the balance that moved. If the bad code is found after lock, it cannot be repaired inside the locked month. The fix is recorded in the close period in which it is discovered, not in the locked period. Once the period is locked, no adjustment, correction, or new entry is made to that period's records, whatever the reason.

If the books cannot be finished in time for the 5th workday, an authorized role tells the finance manager before that deadline. The same notice is required when an unauthorized job ID or a coding dispute is still open as the deadline nears. The finance manager decides whether to clear the issue before lock or to note it for the next period. That decision, on the lock, on unauthorized entries, and on unresolved coding disputes, is final for the period. The date of lock, Dana Okonkwo's approval on form MEC-3, and any unauthorized lines or bad codes caught before lock are written on the close record. Anything deferred to the next period is kept with that record for audit.
